How capture works

Stability-aware capture, not a stopwatch.

An Offscreen Document — not the popup — drives the whole capture, so closing the popup mid-scroll can't kill it.

01

Scroll one viewport, then wait

A content script scrolls the page and starts watching for DOM mutations — new images, injected feed items, layout shifts from lazy content.

02

Capture only once it's quiet

The tile is captured after 150ms with zero mutations — capped at a 2-second max wait, so a page that never fully settles can't hang the capture forever.

quiet ≥ 150ms · hard cap 2000ms per step
03

Stitch in an Offscreen Document

Canvas work happens off the popup entirely, in a document that survives you clicking away. Pages taller than Chrome's ~32,767px canvas ceiling are tiled into numbered parts instead of truncating.

04

Sticky headers cropped, not repeated

Fixed/sticky nav bars are detected once and cropped out of every tile after the first, so your header doesn't repeat down the whole page.

Before you save

Redaction that actually removes the pixels.

A black box drawn over data still has that data underneath it — recoverable by anyone who inspects the file. SnapFull's redact tool paints straight onto the capture canvas, overwriting the source pixels for good.
